02Pain point

Verification still moves
at expert speed.

Real-world deployment has someone accountable for the consequences and the cost. But the algorithms generating these plans live inside environments of their own abstraction — systematically blind to the mechanisms that decide success: queueing, degradation, policy constraints, the things omitted to make the problem tractable.

Today's only remedy is artisanal: consultants and engineering teams hand-building a simulation environment, case by case. Expensive and slow — so verification is rationed to the largest projects at their macro-approval moments. Academia, bearing no deployment responsibility, simply normalized comparing algorithms inside self-built environments.

Responsibility hasn't shrunk. Machine-generated decisions are compounding. Verification still moves at expert speed. That gap is what we build for.

03Solution

Mass-produce the environment,
not the report.

With AI, the work consultants and engineers did case by case can be industrialized for the first time — and what gets mass-produced is not the report, but the environment.

An expert's work decomposes into four parts, three of them text-shaped and exactly what AI collapses: crawling and extracting policy, tariffs, and permitting rules, every value with its citation; learning from prior cases and real deployment outcomes which dimensions a class of decisions actually turns on; selecting the right engine and acceptance standards per problem. The fourth — the physics inside the simulation, and the verdict — stays with first principles and cross-validation, never with a language model: we sell traceability, so every number we extract must itself be traceable.

Environment cost falls from expert-months to days, and verification reaches every decision instead of the top one percent.

05Business model

Per submission,
fixed price.

A plan enters, an evidence package comes back, the fee is fixed at scoping. A revised plan is a new submission — the structural firewall against sliding into open-ended consulting.

Deliberately no subscription: the largest incentive programs reimburse one-time deliverables and exclude monthly fees. Our pricing is shaped by how our customers' money actually arrives.

In this category gross margin is bimodal, and one variable decides it: does marginal labor grow with volume? Our cost lives in the environment, not the engagement — so cost per evaluation is a reported metric, not a promise.
